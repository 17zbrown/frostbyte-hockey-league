Dress it

Saving a line dresses nobody

A saved line is a plan. A game gets a lineup only when you dress it — and that is where the league’s checks run: the weekly cap, suspensions, availability, and each game’s 30-minute lock.

5

Point each game night at a line

In the Night plan, use each night’s Dresses picker — Wednesday, Thursday and Friday, three games each. A toast confirms it: “Thursday dresses Shutdown.” Empty lines stay disabled until you fill them.

6

Dress the night, or the whole week

Dress 3 games files that night; Dress the week (3) files all nine. Confirm, and each dressing runs the checks — anything refused is reported by night and the rest still land. Redress any night to change it before its lock. Rule 5.2

Per-game

One game, one lineup

The per-game page adjusts a single game. Reach it from the builder’s links or the game chips. Fill it from your plan, auto-fill, or set it by hand — then submit.

7

Open the game and fill it

Nine chips, one per game, so games that lock at different times are reachable on their own. Fill from Top Six drops in the line the plan points at; a player who marked himself out is dressed anyway and named in the toast, so you can decide. Availability is your planning tool, not a gate: you may dress anyone on the roster. Auto-fill best available takes each player at his own position first, and Clear empties it.

After the lock

Emergency call-up only

Each game locks 30 minutes before its puck drop. After that only management can change it, only as an emergency call-up, and only until ten minutes after puck drop — every player swapped costs the club one in-game penalty, and the other club is told the moment you submit.

10

The bar turns to “Status: Locked · locked 8:30 PM ET (Rule 5.3).” Every slot reads OVR · locked, and the only control left is Emergency call-up. Rule 5.3

The limits

Spread the games

The weekly cap is the only weekly rule, and it is a ceiling, not a quota. The number that matters most is 16 — the games a player needs to be dressed in the playoffs.

6games a week — every active-roster player, skater or goaltender (Rule 5.2)
3games a week — a training-camp player, at any position (Rule 5.2)
4games of a playoff series — every player (Rule 8.3)
16regular-season games to be dressed in the playoffs (Season 1's published floor) — the Road to 16 card on your Roster page tracks it (Rule 8.3)
How the six is counted

The box score keeps the count

The site tracks every player’s week for you, from what actually happened, and stops you at six.

12

Played games count by the box score

When a game goes final and its box score is imported, the box score is the record: if a player appeared, that game counts toward his week; if he was dressed but sat, it does not.

13

Games still to come count by your filed lineup

Every game you have dressed him for that has not been played yet counts too — so six filed games use up his week even before Friday.

14

At six, the site says no

Try to assign him to a seventh game and the per-game page refuses on the spot: “‹player› has already played or been dressed in 6 games this week — the limit is 6 (Rule 5.2).” Dressing a night or the week refuses that game the same way and lands the rest. In the playoffs the same count runs against the 4-game series cap.

15

After the game, the box score is checked

A player who appears beyond his six, or who appears without being in the lineup you filed, is reported to the league office automatically and dealt with under Rule 5.3 — so play the sheet you filed.

Refusedthe seventh assignment, the seventh filing, and any box score that shows more
Watch outAfter the lock the opponent already sees your sheet. An emergency call-up costs one in-game penalty per player changed — swap two and that is two penalties; moving the same six between positions is none (Rule 5.3). The door closes ten minutes after puck drop: from then the sheet on file is the record, and a player who dresses without being on it is reviewed by the officials against the box score. With three games a night, each locks on its own puck drop: games two and three can lock before game one is done, so reach them by their own chip.
Good to knowThe six-game week is checked when a player is assigned or dressed, not when a line is saved, so a player can sit on all three lines and you dress the mix — the count behind it is his box scores plus your filed lineups. A playoff lineup with a player short of the season's floor (16 games in Season 1) is refused outright, so use the regular season to get your preferred players there.
